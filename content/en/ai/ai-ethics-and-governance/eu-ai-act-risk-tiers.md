# The EU AI Act and risk tiers

The EU AI Act is a risk-based regulatory framework. It regulates AI systems according
to the context and risk of the use case, with additional obligations for
general-purpose AI models.

## Risk tiers

| Tier | Practical meaning |
|---|---|
| Unacceptable risk | prohibited practices such as certain manipulative or social scoring uses |
| High risk | strict obligations for uses affecting safety or fundamental rights |
| Transparency risk | users must be informed about certain AI interactions or generated content |
| Minimal or no risk | no major AI Act obligations, though voluntary codes may apply |
| GPAI | model-provider obligations, with extra duties for systemic-risk models |

The same model can land in different risk contexts depending on how it is used.

## High-risk engineering implications

- Risk management system.
- Data governance and quality controls.
- Technical documentation and logging.
- Transparency and instructions for deployers.
- Human oversight.
- Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity measures.
- Post-market monitoring and incident handling.

## GPAI notes

As of June 2026, official EU guidance states GPAI provider obligations include
technical documentation, copyright policy, and a public training-content summary, with
additional risk management, incident reporting, and cybersecurity duties for systemic
risk models.

## Pitfall

Do not classify only the model. Classify the system, role, users, purpose, decision
impact, and deployment context. This is legal and compliance-sensitive work, so involve
qualified counsel for real obligations.

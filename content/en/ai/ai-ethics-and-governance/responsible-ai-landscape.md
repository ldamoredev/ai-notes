# Responsible AI landscape

Responsible AI is the practice of deciding what should be built, how it should behave,
who it affects, and who remains accountable. It is not a separate layer after modeling;
it is embedded in data, design, evaluation, deployment, and monitoring.

## Core dimensions

| Dimension | Engineering question |
|---|---|
| Fairness | who benefits, who is harmed, and how do outcomes differ by group? |
| Privacy | what data is collected, inferred, stored, shared, and retained? |
| Transparency | can users and reviewers understand the system's role and limits? |
| Safety | can the system cause harm through misuse, failure, or overreach? |
| Accountability | who owns decisions, incidents, appeals, and monitoring? |
| Social impact | how does deployment change work, access, power, and trust? |

## Lifecycle view

- Data collection defines whose reality is represented.
- Labels and objectives define what the system optimizes.
- Metrics define what improvement means.
- UX defines how people rely on or challenge the system.
- Governance defines who can approve, pause, audit, or roll back deployment.

## Governance artifacts

- Use case inventory.
- Risk classification.
- Data and model documentation.
- Evaluation and bias reports.
- Human oversight plan.
- Incident response and appeal process.

## Pitfall

Responsible AI fails when it becomes a values statement without owners, thresholds, or
release gates. Treat it as an operating discipline.
